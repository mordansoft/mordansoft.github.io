Beanjar — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 12 November 2026. Last updated: 14 November 2026.

This policy explains what the Beanjar app (Android package and iOS bundle com.mordansoft.kidtasks, published under the MordanSoft name, here "the app") does with personal data. Beanjar is a family app: a parent sets tasks and rewards, a child does the tasks on their own device. Because children use it, this policy is written to be read by a parent before they connect a child's phone.

The short version
  • A child never creates an account: no email, no password, no phone number, no real name required — a nickname the parent chooses is enough.
  • We ask for no advertising identifier, show no ads and sell or share no personal data. There are no third-party advertising or tracking SDKs in the app.
  • Family data lives on the devices and in our database in the European Union; only the family's own devices can read it.
  • The parent can delete everything from inside the app, at any time, in one action.
  • Beanjar is free. There are no purchases, no subscriptions and no payment data.

2. Who uses the app, and how a parent consents

Beanjar is designed to be set up and controlled by a parent or guardian (an adult). The parent creates the only account in the family, adds children, and writes the tasks, the points and the rewards. A child uses the app on a device that the parent connects by showing a QR code on their own phone; the child's device signs in anonymously and is tied to that family, with no personal details asked of the child anywhere in the app.

By connecting a child's device, the parent gives, on the child's behalf, consent to the processing of the child's data described in this policy. The parent reviews what will be processed before doing it: the app asks for nothing about the child except a nickname and an avatar shape they choose themselves.

The parent may withdraw that consent at any time, without losing the rest of the app: disconnecting the child's device (section 9) stops the collection, and deleting the account erases what was collected. Beanjar does not ask for, and does not condition any feature on, more information about a child than the ticket of the day needs to work.

We do not ask for a child's age or date of birth, and the app is not directed at children using it without a parent: a child's device only ever shows the family a parent set up.

4. What the app deliberately does not do

  • No advertising. The app shows no ads and contains no advertising or attribution SDK. The Android advertising-ID permission is explicitly removed from the app, so no advertising identifier is read, and on iOS the app never asks for permission to track. We do not build profiles and do not track anyone across other apps or websites.
  • No selling or sharing. We do not sell personal data and do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, in the meaning those words have under the CCPA/CPRA or any other law.
  • No location, no contacts, no photo library, no microphone, no health data, no biometrics, no files. The app asks for none of them.
  • No purchases. Beanjar is free; there is no payment, subscription or billing data of any kind.
  • No social features. Nothing a child writes or ticks is visible to anyone outside their own family, there is no chat with strangers, no public profile and no content shared to the internet.
  • No marketing email. A parent's address is used to sign in and to reset a password; we do not send newsletters.
  • No other network traffic. Typefaces and every other resource the app draws are built into it. Apart from the providers in section 7, the app contacts no servers at all.

5. Camera and QR codes

The camera is used for one thing: reading the QR code shown on the screen of another device, to connect a child's phone or to let a second parent join the family. The permission is asked for at that moment and may be declined — everything else in the app keeps working. Camera images are processed on the device as they are scanned, and are never stored, uploaded or sent anywhere. A QR code works once and stops working 15 minutes after it was shown.

6. Notifications

The app sends notifications between the family's own devices — a parent is told that a task was ticked or a reward was asked for, a child is told that a day was confirmed or sent back, and a reminder can arrive before a task is due. To deliver them, the text of the notification, which may contain the child's nickname and a task or reward title, passes through Firebase Cloud Messaging and, on iPhones, the Apple Push Notification service. Reminders set by a parent are scheduled on the child's own device and do not travel over the network at all.

Notifications are optional. If permission is refused or turned off in the system settings, everything else in the app keeps working.

7. Where data is stored, and who processes it for us

Data is written on the device first and then synchronised through Google Firebase. Our database is located in the European Union (Cloud Firestore multi-region eur3, Belgium and the Netherlands) and our server code runs in Belgium (europe-west1). The following providers process data on our behalf, under their data processing terms:

  • Google Firebase — Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Cloud Functions, Cloud Messaging, Crashlytics and Google Analytics for Firebase (Firebase privacy and security).
  • Google Sign-In and Sign in with Apple, if the parent chooses one of them to sign in.
  • Apple Push Notification service, for notifications on iPhones and iPads.

These providers may process some data outside the European Economic Area, including in the United States. Such transfers are covered by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses included in Google's and Apple's data processing terms. We disclose personal data to no one else, except where the law obliges us to.

8. Who can see a family's data

Access is decided on the server, not in the app. The rules let a family's records be read only by the parents of that family and by the devices connected to its children, and a child's device can read only the tasks and records of that one child. A second parent sees the family only after joining with a one-time code shown by the first parent. Removing a device's record is what cuts its access off.

Data travels encrypted (TLS) and is stored encrypted at rest by Google. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affects personal data we will notify the people and the authorities concerned as the law requires.

9. Keeping data, and deleting it

  • Family data is kept for as long as the parent's account exists, because the app's whole purpose is showing the family its own history.
  • Delete account (Family tab → Delete account) erases the family's children, tasks, records, rewards, device links and notification tokens from our servers and deletes the parent's sign-in account. It cannot be undone. A parent who had joined someone else's family is removed from it instead, and that family stays with the other parent.
  • Disconnect devices, on a child's card, cuts that device off from the family's data and deletes its notification token. This is also how a parent withdraws consent for a child while keeping the rest of the family.
  • Removing a child, a task or a reward hides it in the app; the records behind it are erased when the account is deleted.
  • Uninstalling the app removes the local copy on that device. It does not by itself delete the account — use Delete account, or write to us.
  • Crash reports and usage events are kept under the retention settings of Google's services (crash reports up to 90 days; analytics events up to 14 months) and are not linked to an email address, a name or a nickname.
  • We also delete data on request: write to mordansoft@gmail.com from the parent's email address.

10. Your rights

Wherever you live, you may ask us to give you a copy of the personal data we hold, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict or object to its processing, and to withdraw a consent you gave. A parent exercises all of these rights on behalf of their child, and may at any time ask to review what we hold about their child or to have it deleted — including by refusing to let us keep collecting it, with the app still working for the rest of the family.

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, these are your rights under the GDPR and the UK GDPR, and you may also complain to your national data protection authority. If you are in California, the CCPA/CPRA gives you the rights to know, to delete, to correct and to opt out of sale or sharing — and, as section 4 says, we neither sell nor share personal information, so there is nothing to opt out of. Exercising any right never makes the app worse for you.

To use a right, write to mordansoft@gmail.com.

12. Children's privacy

Beanjar is a family app, used by children with their parent's knowledge and under their parent's control. We follow the principle of collecting the minimum: the app asks for nothing about a child except a nickname chosen by the parent and the record of tasks done. We show children no advertising, do not profile them, do not use their data to target anything, and do not disclose it to anyone outside their own family.

Our practices are intended to meet the United States Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), Art. 8 GDPR, the UK Age Appropriate Design Code, and the Google Play Families policy and Apple's rules for apps used by children. We obtain the parent's consent before any data about a child is processed, as part of the parent connecting the child's device; this policy is the notice of what that consent covers.

If you believe a child's data reached us without a parent's consent, write to mordansoft@gmail.com and we will delete it.
