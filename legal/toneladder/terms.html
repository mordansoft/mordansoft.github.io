What Toneladder is — and what it is not

Toneladder trains relative pitch: a cadence sets a key, a note plays, you name the scale degree, and a wrong answer gets an explanation. It has four levels, from “is this note home?” to the whole major scale, and a journal of your accuracy.

Toneladder does not train or promise absolute pitch — naming notes with no reference — and it makes no promise that you will reach any particular level of hearing, in any period of time. Progress depends on you and on how you practise.

It is also not a hearing test and not medical advice. Toneladder does not measure, assess, diagnose or treat your hearing, and it is not a substitute for an audiologist, a doctor or a music teacher. If you think something is wrong with your hearing, see a professional rather than an app.

Volume and your ears

The app plays notes and chords, and you will often use headphones. Set the volume at a comfortable level and turn it down if a lesson is uncomfortable: listening loudly, especially through headphones, can damage hearing over time. If you hear nothing, use Check sound in Settings before you reach for the volume.
